\section{Results}

\subsection{Municipal prediction accuracy}
Table~\ref{tab:main-metrics} summarizes validation and test metrics for the proposed MSTNet municipal predictor.
Validation $R^2$ peaks at epoch 12 ($0.424$), with RMSE $0.313$\,\si{t/ha}.
The corresponding test metrics are $R^2=0.398$ and RMSE $0.309$\,\si{t/ha}.
The validation and test results are similar, with errors near $0.31$\,\si{t/ha} on the fixed 2021 holdout split.

Split & RMSE & MAE & $R^2$ \\
\midrule
Validation (2021) & 0.313 & 0.242 & 0.424 \\
Test (2021)       & 0.309 & 0.237 & 0.398 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Figure~\ref{fig:learning} shows learning dynamics.
Validation RMSE and $R^2$ improve through epoch 12. After that, validation fluctuates (no learning-rate drop falls in this window), and training stops early at epoch 27 after fifteen epochs without a new validation $R^2$ best.

\subsection{Auxiliary comparisons}
\label{sec:ablations}

\paragraph{Spectral-only reference.}
The top spectral-only configuration achieves validation $R^2=0.165$ (RMSE $0.376$\,\si{t/ha}) and test $R^2=0.117$ (RMSE $0.375$\,\si{t/ha}).
MSTNet is stronger on this split, but the experiments also differ in architecture and optimization, not only in which covariates are available. Attributing the gap to climate or soil would need a controlled modality ablation with the same network and training protocol.

\paragraph{Temporal pooling.}
With the best spectral-only architecture fixed, replacing NDVI-weighted temporal pooling by mean pooling over valid timesteps reduces validation $R^2$ from $0.165$ to $0.049$ (best epoch 12), while test $R^2$ remains nearly unchanged ($0.117$).
NDVI-weighted pooling improves validation fit in this spectral-only comparison, but this improvement does not translate into a clear test gain on the single holdout year.

\subsection{Per-year municipal diagnostics}
Figure~\ref{fig:scatter} shows predicted versus observed municipal productivity by harvest year.
The 2021 value ($R^2=0.395$) agrees closely with the checkpoint's held-out test metric.
In 2022, $R^2$ is $0.612$ but RMSE increases to $0.709$\,\si{t/ha}. This combination is possible because $R^2$ measures relative variation around that year's mean, while RMSE measures errors in the original productivity scale. Results for 2023--2024 are intermediate (Table~\ref{tab:per-year}).
