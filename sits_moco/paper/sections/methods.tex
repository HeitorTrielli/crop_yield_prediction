\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

We first describe the MSTNet architecture (STNet trunks and late-fusion head), then how the network is trained under municipal PAM labels and scored at pixel level after training, and finally the loss.

\subsection{MSTNet architecture}
\label{sec:architecture}
MSTNet (\emph{Multi} STNet) places one STNet trunk on each time-varying stream so that modalities with different sampling rates need not share a common calendar.
The single-trunk STNet material and notation below follow~\citet{xu2024sitsmoco}; their encoder builds on the self-attention architecture for satellite time series of~\citet{russwurm2020selfattention}.
We add trunk indices $s$ and $c$ for the dual-stream case and replace the classification head by a regression decoder with late soil fusion.
Figure~\ref{fig:mstnet-arch} summarizes the network.

\paragraph{Sequential encoding.}
Write a satellite imagery time series (SITS) as $\mathbf{X}=[\mathbf{x}_1,\ldots,\mathbf{x}_T]$ with $\mathbf{x}_t\in\mathbb{R}^{C}$.
In MSTNet the spectral trunk takes $\mathbf{X}_s$ with $C_s=10$ Sentinel-2 bands and length $T_s=45$, the number of clear observations in the municipality with the densest Sentinel-2 series over the Oct--Mar window.
Shorter municipal series are zero-padded and masked to this length.
The climate trunk takes $\mathbf{X}_c$ with $C_c=5$ climate channels over the Oct--Mar window.
That window has 183 days in leap years, when February has 29 days, and 182 days otherwise, so we fix $T_c=183$ and pad ordinary seasons by one masked step.
Each timestep is mapped by a trunk-specific, weight-sharing MLP,
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{e}_{i,t} = MLP_i(\mathbf{x}_{i,t}),
  \label{eq:mlp-step}
\end{equation}
and the trunk embedding matrix stacks these vectors as
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{E}_i=[\mathbf{e}_{i,1},\ldots,\mathbf{e}_{i,T_i}]\in\mathbb{R}^{T_i\times d},
  \label{eq:mlp-encode}
\end{equation}
where $i\in\{s,c\}$.
Because self-attention is permutation-equivariant, a day-of-year (DoY) positional vector $\mathbf{p}_{i,t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ is added at every step,
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{e}_{i,t}' = \mathbf{e}_{i,t} + \mathbf{p}_{i,t},
  \label{eq:doy-add}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{p}_{i,t}
  =
  \bigl[\sin(\mathrm{DoY}_{i,t}/\tau^{2k/d}),\,
        \cos(\mathrm{DoY}_{i,t}/\tau^{2k/d})\bigr]_{k=1}^{d/2},
  \label{eq:doy-pe}
\end{equation}
and $\tau=1000$, which gives $\mathbf{E}_i'=[\mathbf{e}_{i,1}',\ldots,\mathbf{e}_{i,T_i}']$.
The DoY calendars differ by trunk: spectral steps follow the clear Sentinel-2 acquisition dates, while climate steps follow the daily Oct--Mar grid.

\paragraph{Transformer encoder.}
Each trunk applies a Transformer encoder to $\mathbf{E}_i'$.
Inside each encoder layer, multi-head self-attention forms query, key, and value projections at every timestep, runs scaled dot-product attention over the sequence in parallel heads, and recombines the head outputs.
A position-wise feed-forward network follows, and both sublayers use residual connections with layer normalization, yielding $\mathbf{O}_i=[\mathbf{o}_{i,1},\ldots,\mathbf{o}_{i,T_i}]\in\mathbb{R}^{T_i\times d}$.

\paragraph{Seq2Vec.}
A Seq2Vec module collapses $\mathbf{O}_i$ to a single vector $\mathbf{o}_i\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$.
On the spectral trunk we use an NDVI-weighted temporal mean: with NIR and red channels of $\mathbf{x}_{s,t}$,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{NDVI}_t
  =
  \frac{x_{s,t}^{\mathrm{NIR}}-x_{s,t}^{\mathrm{Red}}}{x_{s,t}^{\mathrm{NIR}}+x_{s,t}^{\mathrm{Red}}},
  \label{eq:ndvi}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{w}=\mathrm{softmax}\bigl([\mathrm{NDVI}_t]_{t=1}^{T_s}\bigr),
  \label{eq:ndvi-weights}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{o}_s
  =
  \sum_{t=1}^{T_s} w_t\,\mathbf{o}_{s,t},
  \label{eq:ndvi-seq2vec}
\end{equation}
so cloudy or low-vegetation dates receive small weight.
On the climate trunk there is no NDVI analogue; we take the mean of $\mathbf{O}_c$ over valid (unmasked) days, denoted $\mathbf{o}_c$.

\paragraph{Output head.}
Since MapBiomas Solo properties $\mathbf{s}$ are static once the harvest year is known, we fuse them directly in the decoder.
MSTNet concatenates the trunk embeddings with soil and decodes a scalar productivity estimate,
\begin{equation}
  \hat{y}
  =
  MLP_2\bigl([\mathbf{o}_s;\mathbf{o}_c;\mathbf{s}]\bigr),
  \label{eq:output-head}
\end{equation}
with $\hat{y}$ interpreted per pixel or per municipal aggregate according to the input path in Section~\ref{sec:pixel-formulation}.

\subsection{Municipal-mean training and post-training pixel scoring}
\label{sec:pixel-formulation}
Let $\mathcal{P}_{m,y}$ denote the soybean pixels observed in municipality $m$ in harvest year $y$.
Training uses the municipal-mean path: every covariate stream is restricted to those soy pixels and averaged before it enters the network.
For Sentinel-2 reflectance at date $t$,
\begin{equation}
  \bar{\mathbf{x}}_{m,y,t}
  =
  \frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}_{m,y,t}|}
  \sum_{p \in \mathcal{P}_{m,y,t}}\mathbf{x}_{p,t},
  \label{eq:municipal-mean}
\end{equation}
and the same soy-masked average yields the municipal climate series $\bar{\mathbf{c}}_{m,y}$ and soil vector $\bar{\mathbf{s}}_{m,y}$.
Each municipality--year is then one forward pass
$\hat{y}_{m,y}^{\mathrm{train}}=f_\theta(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{m,y},\bar{\mathbf{c}}_{m,y},\bar{\mathbf{s}}_{m,y})$
supervised by PAM (Figure~\ref{fig:data-flow}).

After training, the same weights define a productivity score $\hat{y}_p$ for each soy pixel $p$ from its Sentinel-2 sequence $\mathbf{x}_p$, the municipal daily climate series $\mathbf{c}_{m,y}$ (broadcast to all pixels in $m$), and soil covariates $\mathbf{s}_p$.
Each pixel score is
\begin{equation}
  \hat{y}_p = f_\theta(\mathbf{x}_p, \mathbf{c}_{m,y}, \mathbf{s}_p),
  \label{eq:pixel-score}
\end{equation}
and municipal predictions average those scores,
\begin{equation}
  \hat{y}_{m,y}
  =
  \frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}_{m,y}|}
  \sum_{p \in \mathcal{P}_{m,y}}
  \hat{y}_p ,
  \label{eq:pixel-mean-agg}
\end{equation}
then compared with the PAM label $y_{m,y}$.
The same field $\{\hat{y}_p\}$ can be kept as a map or averaged over any sub-municipal polygon.
This post-training pixel path is the source of the LOO metrics, maps, and scatterplots in Section~\ref{sec:results}.
Appendix~\ref{app:pixel-pipeline} diagrams the corresponding data pipeline and recalls the shared MSTNet architecture at pixel scoring time.
The same forward path would be used to train pixel-wise by scoring every soy pixel and averaging before the loss; we do not train that way because it is much slower while targeting the same municipal labels.
Because $f_\theta$ is nonlinear, mean-then-encode and encode-then-mean need not coincide.
Section~\ref{sec:exp-muni-mean} reports the same LOO years under the municipal-mean path.

\subsection{Loss}
\label{sec:loss}
Let $y_{m,y}$ be the PAM productivity for municipality $m$ in harvest year $y$, and let $\mu$ and $\sigma$ be the mean and standard deviation of PAM targets on the training years of the current fold.
Targets are standardized as
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{y}_{m,y}
  =
  \frac{y_{m,y}-\mu}{\sigma},
  \label{eq:target-standardize}
\end{equation}
and the municipal-mean forward pass outputs a prediction $\hat{y}_{m,y}^{\mathrm{train}}$ in the same standardized space.
Training minimizes the mean squared error over the municipal-mean training set $\mathcal{D}$,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}(\theta)
  =
  \frac{1}{|\mathcal{D}|}
  \sum_{(m,y)\in\mathcal{D}}
  \bigl(\hat{y}_{m,y}^{\mathrm{train}}-\tilde{y}_{m,y}\bigr)^2.
  \label{eq:mse-loss}
\end{equation}
Within each leave-one-year-out fold, $\mu$, $\sigma$, and the channel-wise standardization statistics for Sentinel-2, climate, and soil are recomputed using only the four training years and then applied unchanged to the held-out year.
Reported metrics (RMSE, MAE, and $R^2$) use the inverse map $\sigma\hat{y}+\mu$ back to \si{t/ha}.
