\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented MSTNet, a multi-frequency Transformer that learns soybean productivity from municipal PAM labels and scores soy pixels for sub-municipal maps.
Sentinel-2 time series and daily Xavier climate enter parallel STNet trunks; MapBiomas Solo enters the decoder by late fusion.
Training uses soy-masked municipal means for speed; after training, the same weights score every soy pixel and average to municipalities or arbitrary polygons.

Year leave-one-out on Paran\'{a} harvest years 2020--2024 is the primary score.
Excluding the 2022 drought fold, municipal-mean LOO reaches mean $R^2=0.275$ and the pixel path reaches mean $R^2=0.302$ (RMSE $=0.427$\,\si{t/ha}), slightly above the municipal-mean path on the same checkpoints.
Partial-season scoring approaches that full-window accuracy by February on non-drought folds, allowing an early prediction of that year's yield.
Fixed-architecture modality ablations rank climate first, Sentinel-2 second, and soil last on ordinary years; soil can help typical seasons and hurt the drought fold.
Under an 80/20 municipality holdout with shared seasons instead of year-level split, pooled $R^2=0.736$, though much of that pooled score separates years rather than places within a year.

The 2022 LOO collapse is a missing-regime problem, not evidence that drought is intrinsically hard for these covariates: when 2022 appears on both sides of a spatial split it becomes the strongest year, and we expect drought years to become easier once stress seasons are present in training, while favourable seasons remain harder for within-year ranking.
Intramunicipal maps stay qualitative until field yields can score them.

Further work follows the discussion in Section~\ref{sec:discussion-encoder-decoder}.
On the data side, we are still lacking broader geographic and drought coverage and pixel-path evaluation at multi-state scale, so stress regimes are represented in training rather than left as LOO surprises.
On the model side, within-year ranking or correlation losses, a year-level baseline plus municipal deviation, generative auxiliary tasks, and a stress-aware climate Seq2Vec should push the stack beyond seasonal-level fit, while adding standard vegetation indices and climate stress summaries as explicit features will test whether hand-crafted cues still help when the raw series are already present.
