\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Reliable soybean productivity estimates matter for agricultural monitoring, insurance, and risk assessment.
In Brazil, the Municipal Agricultural Production survey (PAM) from the Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics (IBGE) publishes official yields only at the municipality level. The same kind of coarse reporting is common elsewhere.
Sentinel-2 imagery, gridded weather, and soil maps describe crop condition at much finer scales, but field-level yield labels are rarely available statewide.
What we need is a model that can learn from municipal labels and still make predictions for smaller regions, at a scale that is cheap enough to run over large areas.

MSTNet (\emph{Multi} STNet) is our answer to that requirement. It extends the Spectral--Temporal Network (STNet) of~\citet{xu2024sitsmoco} by giving each covariate stream its own trunk when the streams are sampled at different frequencies.
Here, one trunk encodes soy-masked Sentinel-2 reflectance and another encodes daily climate. Annual MapBiomas Solo properties, which are static once the harvest year is fixed, are concatenated in the decoder.
Predictions are defined per soy pixel.
For speed, training fits the network on municipality-mean Sentinel-2 series supervised by PAM.
At evaluation and inference we apply the same weights to every soy pixel and average the scores to municipalities (or to any other polygon) without retraining.

We run the experiments on soybean municipalities in Paran\'{a} for harvest years 2020--2024 and hold out 2021 for validation and test.
Because PAM is the only supervised target, the numbers we report are municipal: holdout $R^2$, RMSE, and related diagnostics on municipal aggregates.
We are not aware of many yield studies that train a multi-frequency Transformer on municipal labels while still exposing pixel scores for downscaling. Matched baselines for that setup are scarce, so we present the municipal holdout as a reproducible benchmark rather than a direct comparison against the literature.

Our contributions are MSTNet itself, the pixel-scoring setup that turns municipal supervision into sub-municipal maps, and a fixed 2021 municipality holdout for Paran\'{a} over 2020--2024.
