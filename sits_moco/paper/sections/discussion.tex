\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

On the 2021 municipality holdout, validation and test stay close: RMSE is about $0.31$\,\si{t/ha}, with $R^2=0.424$ on validation and $R^2=0.398$ on test. That agreement suggests the selected epoch did not overfit the validation partition hard, but it says little about other years.

MSTNet beats the separately tuned spectral-only reference on this split. The gap is interesting, yet we cannot pin it on climate or soil alone: the two experiments also differ in architecture and optimization. A fairer ablation would freeze those choices and add climate and soil one at a time.

The spectral-only search preferred a small Transformer, while MSTNet uses a larger two-trunk design. Together with the validation swings after epoch 12, that points to capacity and optimization as first-order factors. Repeated seeds or a nested selection protocol would make that sensitivity clearer.

The 2022 numbers show why $R^2$ alone is not enough. Cross-municipality $R^2$ reaches $0.612$, but RMSE climbs to $0.709$\,\si{t/ha}, well above the 2021 level. Operational reports should keep both absolute error and explained variance, and they should call out drought years explicitly.

In the spectral-only architecture, NDVI-weighted temporal pooling raised validation $R^2$ relative to mean pooling, while test $R^2$ barely moved. That looks more like a model-selection effect than a reliable generalization gain.

Because scores are defined per pixel, municipal PAM labels can in principle support downscaling to arbitrary sub-municipal shapes (Figure~\ref{fig:guara}).
The numbers in this paper evaluate municipal aggregates of those pixel scores; they do not check intramunicipal maps against field yields.
Training itself used municipality-mean Sentinel-2 series for speed, so the reported skill is a transfer check of those weights under encode-then-mean evaluation.
We also have only one holdout year, no repeated runs, few matched external baselines for this training setup, and clear sensitivity to unusual seasons.
Next steps are multi-year and geographic holdouts, controlled ablations, stronger baselines, and independent yield data when the goal is spatially resolved accuracy.
