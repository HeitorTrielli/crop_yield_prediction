\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The year-LOO scores raise a few interpretation questions before we turn to model limits.
We first place MSTNet against the municipality holdout used by~\citet{song2022soy}, then focus on the 2022 drought fold, and finally discuss where the encoder and decoder may still fall short and what might help.

\subsection{Temporal versus spatial holdout}
\label{sec:discussion-yoo-vs-muni}

Year leave-one-out gives a clearer picture of temporal risk than holding out municipalities while sharing the same harvest years between train and test.
\citet{song2022soy} report both holdouts for Brazilian soybean under IBGE municipal labels.
Their main spatial evaluation randomly holds out 20\% of municipalities while keeping all years in both pools, which measures interpolation under shared seasons rather than a forecast of an unseen year.
Their leave-one-year-out assessment is harder: RMSE ranges from $259$ to $816$\,\si{kg/ha}, with notably weaker years (e.g.\ 2005 and 2015).
A forward 2020 map trained on 2001--2019 reaches $R^2=0.66$ before a post-hoc linear bias correction that uses a small fraction of 2020 municipal labels, so that year is not a pure holdout either.
Our primary scores are year-LOO by design so that held-out seasons never appear in training.
Appendix~\ref{app:muni80-20} reports the same MSTNet under their 80/20 municipality holdout on Paran\'{a}: pooled holdout $R^2=0.736$, with yearly $R^2$ between $0.283$ and $0.636$ and a five-year mean of $0.467$.
That pooled score is comparable to the spatial holdout of~\citet{song2022soy}, but much of it comes from separating years rather than municipalities within a year: when the same predictions are scored year by year, $R^2$ falls well below the pooled figure.
We also evaluate only within Paran\'{a}, so we cannot claim that the model distinguishes largely distinct geographies the way a national fit can.
We do expect, however, that adding more training data---more years, or more states even when their geography differs---would more likely raise than lower performance.
On non-drought folds, the municipal-mean path reaches $R^2$ between $0.20$ and $0.41$, while municipal aggregates of pixel scores reach $R^2$ between $0.23$ and $0.40$.
Excluding 2022, the pooled means are $R^2=0.275$ for municipal-mean evaluation and $R^2=0.302$ for the pixel path.
Those year-LOO magnitudes sit closer to the temporal assessment of~\citet{song2022soy} than to a spatial holdout with shared seasons, as expected for a five-year Paran\'{a} panel rather than a national nineteen-year fit.

Those two routes are not the same estimator.
Municipal-mean LOO applies $f_\theta$ to soy-masked averages of the inputs (mean-then-encode), whereas the pixel path scores each soy pixel and averages the outputs (encode-then-mean).
Since $f_\theta$ is nonlinear, predicting from municipal means need not match the mean of pixel-wise predictions.
The slightly higher pooled $R^2$ on the pixel path suggests that post-training downscaling can recover municipal signal that is smoothed away when covariates are pre-averaged.

Training on the encode-then-mean path would align checkpoint selection with pixel inference and sub-municipal maps, but we kept municipal-mean training for practical reasons.
Each municipality--year is one training example (municipalities times training years per LOO fold), and the full fold fits in memory.
Pixel-wise training would run a forward and backward pass on every soy pixel in those municipality--years before municipal averaging, which amounts to hundreds of millions of pixel updates per epoch.
The network fits in GPU memory; the bottleneck is reading pixel time series from disk in batches.
That I/O load made pixel-wise training too costly relative to municipal-mean fitting, so we train mean-then-encode and report pixel-path LOO on the same checkpoints.

\subsection{The 2022 drought year}
\label{sec:discussion-drought-2022}

The 2022 fold is the exception that matters for operations.
It is the only drought year in the Paran\'{a} sample, so leaving it out removes every training example from that regime.
The resulting negative $R^2$ under both evaluation paths shows that the present covariates and capacity do not invent drought behaviour from wetter years alone.
When 2022 remains in training and another year is held out, LOO $R^2$ is much more stable.
This gap is mainly a data problem.
If the training set includes municipalities that suffered poor yields in other years and regions, drought-like response patterns become observable rather than unique to one left-out season.\footnote{We have already begun that expansion by adding Rio Grande do Sul, S\~{a}o Paulo, and Goi\'{a}s to the municipal-mean pipeline. On that multi-state year LOO setup we obtain mean $R^2$ about $0.46$, with fold scores ranging from about $0.2$ to $0.7$. We do not report those numbers as main results here because the pixel-by-pixel pipeline has not yet been run at that scale, and municipal-mean scores alone would miss the downscaling objective that motivates MSTNet.}

Leave-one-modality-out runs on the same year-LOO folds show that climate carries most of the non-drought signal, Sentinel-2 less, and soil least.
Omitting climate costs about $0.21$ in mean pixel-path $R^2$ when 2022 is excluded, versus about $0.10$ without Sentinel-2 and about $0.06$ without soil.
Soil still matters for interpretation: texture and SOC are nearly static across years, so they act as a spatial productivity prior that helps typical seasons but can worsen the drought fold.
Without soil the 2022 LOO score rises from a large negative $R^2$ to near zero, while ordinary years still favor the full three-stream model.
Ranking modalities by the mean that includes 2022 can therefore look misleading; the fair comparison for typical seasons is the mean that excludes that fold.

\subsection{Where the encoder and decoder may still struggle}
\label{sec:discussion-encoder-decoder}
Beyond the LOO scores above, a natural worry for this setup is that municipal MSE can be reduced by getting the seasonal level roughly right while leaving municipalities poorly ordered within a year.
Climate already carries most of the non-drought signal, so the trunks may prefer year-level structure over the smaller within-year productivity residual that PAM still varies about the seasonal mean.
The decoder may then shrink forecasts toward a mid-range mode: a narrow MLP with heavy dropout has little incentive under plain MSE to spread predictions when residual signal is noisy, and the same head must jointly learn cross-year calibration and within-municipality ranking.
Drought LOO makes that conflict sharper, because absolute level and residual ordering are asked of one scalar pathway with no analogous season in training.
There may also be a signal ceiling: management, cultivar, planting date, and field history are absent, and soy-masked municipal means discard within-municipality heterogeneity that could distinguish productive from marginal soy areas inside the same PAM unit.
Those are hypotheses about where capacity and supervision may be misallocated, not claims that any one of them is already proven.

\subsection{Ideas for the encoder}
\label{sec:discussion-improve-encoder}
If within-year residual productivity is under-represented, the encoder may need supervision that asks for it explicitly.
An auxiliary yield probe on the fused embedding would push the trunks to stay linearly (or lightly nonlinearly) predictive of PAM rather than only useful after a deep head.
Within-year ranking or correlation losses over municipalities that share a harvest year would penalize scrambling who is above or below the seasonal mean.
Supervised contrastive or ordinal objectives on within-year yield terciles would go further by pulling same-tercile municipalities together and pushing different terciles apart inside each season.
Reconstruction of spectral or climate tokens could help trunks keep raw structure during fine-tuning, but it does not ask for productivity geometry and is unlikely to be enough on its own.
Year-adversarial training looks less attractive here: year and climate structure seem to be the dominant useful signal in the ablations, so erasing it risks removing what the model uses for LOO calibration.
On the input side, distributional municipal summaries (quantiles, variance) or more encode-then-mean training when I/O allows could reduce the information lost by averaging before the trunks.
Attention or phenology-weighted climate pooling might emphasize stress windows better than a uniform seasonal mean.
Static soil might also work better as a gated or residualized spatial prior against the seasonal climate state than as an unconditional concatenation that can help ordinary years and hurt drought LOO.
Broader geographic and drought coverage remains essential either way: trunks cannot learn drought-sensitive features that never appear among the training municipality--years.

\subsection{Ideas for the decoder}
\label{sec:discussion-improve-decoder}
If the head is flattening mid-range forecasts, architectural and objective changes may help.
Wider residual MLPs with a linear skip from the fused vector, milder dropout, and smoother activations would leave more room for residual signal to reach the output.
Modality-specific scores fused late could make climate level, spectral residual, and soil prior easier to combine without one pathway dominating.
Within-year correlation or ranking losses, and penalties against unrealistically small prediction variance inside a year, would push the head to order municipalities rather than collapse them to the training mode.
Predicting a year-level baseline plus a municipal deviation about that baseline would separate drought calibration from within-year ranking.
Mixture or quantile heads are natural if PAM residuals are heavy-tailed across municipalities, because under-dispersion then becomes an explicit modelling failure rather than a silent MSE optimum.
None of this removes the need for regimes that look like the left-out year, or for covariates that capture management and cultivar.
A better decoder can only extract more of whatever residual the encoder and inputs retain.
The useful agenda, in our view, is joint: keep year-level climate structure, add explicit within-year productivity pressure in the encoder, and give the decoder less incentive to flatten the mid-range of the prediction surface.
