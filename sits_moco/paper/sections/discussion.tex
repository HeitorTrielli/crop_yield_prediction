\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Year leave-one-out gives a clearer picture of temporal risk than a single fixed holdout.
On non-drought folds, municipal aggregates of pixel scores reach $R^2$ between $0.23$ and $0.40$, with RMSE about $0.32$--$0.57$\,\si{t/ha}.
The pooled mean excluding 2022 is $R^2=0.302$ and RMSE $=0.427$\,\si{t/ha}.

The 2022 fold is the exception that matters for operations.
It is the only drought year in the sample, so leaving it out removes every training example from that regime.
The resulting negative $R^2$ and RMSE above $1.4$\,\si{t/ha} show that the present covariates and capacity do not invent drought behaviour from wetter years alone.
When 2022 remains in training and another year is held out, LOO skill is much more stable.
Any operational deployment should therefore monitor season type explicitly and avoid treating a single mild year as representative.

Because scores are defined per pixel, municipal PAM labels can still support downscaling to arbitrary sub-municipal shapes (Figure~\ref{fig:guara}).
The numbers here evaluate municipal aggregates of those pixel scores; they do not check intramunicipal maps against field yields.
Training used municipality-mean Sentinel-2 series for speed, so LOO skill is a transfer check under encode-then-mean evaluation.
Other limitations are the single-state geography, no repeated seeds, and scarce matched external baselines.
Next steps are multi-state LOO, controlled modality ablations, stronger baselines, and independent yield data when spatially resolved accuracy is required.
